## Design Specificity Verdict

Specific in its language, generic in its structure - and the structure is what fails the user.

The copy and taxonomy could not be lifted. "How long have you got?" / "How much energy?" with `Gentle / Steady / A stretch` reframes difficulty as capacity today rather than skill level - a real insight, carried consistently through filters, cards, meta rows and collection blurbs.

The composition is a stock content-app template: sticky header with wordmark + three nav items + counter; H1 + sub-paragraph; hero card; `[1fr 20rem]` content-plus-right-rail-filters; card grid. Today and Browse use literally the same grid declaration (`Today.tsx:37`, `Browse.tsx:50`). Swap the nouns and this is a recipe app or a property search. The studio-table metaphor stops at ornament and never reaches structure. Time, the organising constraint, appears only as a chip and a "6 min" label.

**Deterministic scan.** `impeccable detect --json src` = 0 findings, exit 0, no ignore config, no inline suppressions. Control-tested with a planted bad file (fired `bounce-easing`), but the source ruleset caught only 1 of ~7 planted defects - weak evidence, not an a11y pass.

## Overall Impression

A product with a genuinely good idea about people, wrapped in a layout that ignores the scene it was built for. The voice is excellent and the no-pressure commitment is upheld end to end - no streak/score/goal/achievement language anywhere, and `pickDaily` is date-modulo with no state, structurally incapable of tracking a run.

What lets it down is that every hard decision about the phone was deferred to a desktop grid. Biggest opportunity: stop treating the phone as the desktop layout collapsed into one column.

## What's Working

1. **The filter vocabulary is a real design insight, correctly implemented.** Reframing difficulty as energy-today rather than skill removes the self-assessment that stops beginners. Carried consistently through filters, cards, meta rows, collection blurbs.
2. **Reference colour fidelity is enforced structurally.** `RefArt` uses the fixed `--art-mat` neutral with `object-contain`, no filter/blend/opacity. Paper grain at `z-index: 0` behind `main`'s `z-10`. Both agents probed for tinting and found none.
3. **Accessibility fundamentals are solid.** Across 10 route x viewport passes: zero horizontal overflow, zero console errors, zero failed requests, zero missing accessible names, one `h1` per route with no skipped levels, full landmarks with skip link, reduced motion genuinely honoured (0 of 463 elements animating). Alt text describes subjects well enough to decide whether to paint them.

## Priority Issues

No P0. Nothing prevents task completion outright, though the first two come close in the stated usage scene.

### [P1] `--ink-faint` fails AA on the text that drives the core decision

`#837E72` = 3.59:1 on Paper, 3.81:1 on raised surfaces, against 4.5:1. Verified three ways (independent computation, live computed styles, detector URL scan). Used in 12 source locations at 12.48-15.2px, including the three filter questions (`FilterControls.tsx:121`), "SUGGESTED PALETTE" headings, all result counts, the provenance line, the entire footer, inactive nav labels.

Why it matters: DESIGN.md's contrast section verifies Ink, teal, rose and sage but never checks `ink-faint` - it passed review by omission. The failing text is the core decision mechanism, failing in the exact bad-light scene PRODUCT.md describes.

Fix: darken `--ink-faint` to >= 4.5:1 on Paper (around `#6E6A5F`), or promote body-size uses to `--ink-soft` (6.29:1) and reserve `ink-faint` for decorative marks. Add the verified ratio to DESIGN.md's contrast table.

Command: `/impeccable audit`

### [P1] The Enlarge view is smaller than the page it enlarges, and clips in landscape

`EnlargeDialog.tsx:50-55` forces `aspect-[4/3]` with `inset="roomy"` on square artwork in a dialog with no max-height. Measured: detail page renders ~272px of pear, enlarge dialog ~209px - 23% smaller. At 844x390 (propped phone, landscape) the image box computes to 595px inside a 390px viewport; subject clipped.

Why it matters: "an enlarged view useful beside a physical sketchbook" is a stated requirement in PRODUCT.md and CLAUDE.md. It is the final step of the primary journey and the note the experience ends on.

Fix: drop the fixed aspect ratio; size the dialog to the viewport (`max-height: 100dvh; max-width: 100vw`, full-bleed on small screens) and let `object-contain` fit. Reduce `inset` to `snug` or none inside the dialog. Verify art fills >= 80% of the shorter viewport dimension in both orientations.

Command: `/impeccable adapt`

### [P1] On phone, filters sit ~2,500px from the results they filter

`Today.tsx:83-87` and `Browse.tsx:74-78` place the filter `<aside>` after results in DOM order, so the `lg:` sidebar collapses to the bottom. Verified: "Refine" at y=2501 of a 3171px page; below all 12 cards in a 14,008px Browse page. Keyboard users reach it last. Detector reached the same conclusion structurally ("one column running 197% of the viewport tall while a sibling fits in 63%"). Compounding: "Open this piece" at y=855 on an 844px viewport.

Why it matters: hero copy says "Change the mood with the filters" while they are three screens away, and the product's positioning rests on them.

Fix: below `lg`, render filters as a compact summary bar under the H1 ("5 min - Gentle - 2 active") opening a bottom sheet; keep the sidebar at `lg+`. Cap featured art (~`max-h-[45svh]`), reduce the `roomy` inset, lift title/minutes/difficulty above the fold.

Command: `/impeccable layout`

### [P2] You can save a piece, and there is nowhere to look at what you saved

`SaveButton` persists to localStorage; the header shows a count as a non-interactive `<span>` (`DirectionShell.tsx:102-108`); no `/saved` route (`App.tsx:10-20`). `favorites.ts:40-42` also swallows storage write failures, so a save can vanish on reload with no signal.

Why it matters: the action is offered on every card and both hero surfaces with no destination. For the returning hobbyist, saved pieces are the natural way back in on day two.

Fix: ship the saved list behind the header count (make it a `<Link>`), or disclose honestly until it exists. Surface storage write failures.

Command: `/impeccable harden`

### [P2] A CSS class collision leaks sticky-note styling onto the app header

`index.css:174` defines `.sticky` with a note background and drop shadow in `@layer components`, colliding with Tailwind's `.sticky` position utility. Verified at source: the only bare-class user is `<header className="sticky top-0 ...">` (`DirectionShell.tsx:74`), measured in-browser computing the note's drop shadow. The note carries both `absolute` and `sticky`. `StickyNote.tsx:14` hard-codes `246 216 122` (yellow), not a DESIGN.md token, and the loudest colour on the desktop page.

Why it matters: silent, spreading defect - every future `sticky` element inherits note styling. In a product whose third principle is "the artwork carries the colour", the brightest pixel is a decorative yellow rectangle.

Fix: rename to `.note-paper`; add `--note` to the token table in `index.css` and DESIGN.md; give `StickyNote` a single `position`.

Command: `/impeccable polish`

## Persona Red Flags

**The nervous beginner.** Opens to a heading, a paragraph and a large mat with no action visible (CTA y=855, fold 844). "Gentle" is never explained - `DIFFICULTY_NOTE["gentle"] = "Loose shapes, forgiving edges"` is defined at `types.ts:60` and rendered nowhere (confirmed by grep). The one sentence written to give this person permission is dead code. Scrolls past the CTA into "More to try - 11 more" - the decision the product exists to remove, handed back with eleven options and no guidance. Taps Enlarge; the picture gets smaller.

**The returning hobbyist.** Taps "Deal me another"; the featured piece changes with no announcement, no transition and no scroll adjustment (verified: scrollY unchanged at 519). Scrolled past the artwork, they cannot tell anything happened. Saves two pieces; the header reads "2" forever with nowhere to go. For 30-minute pieces, scrolls to y=2501 and back.

**Wet-handed, one-handed, bad light.** Touch targets below 44x44, measured in-browser: header nav links 83-102 x 40 (class is `min-h-[40px]`, 4px short of DESIGN.md's own rule), wordmark link 139 x 32, "Clear N" 54.6 x 32, and on Browse all 12 piece-title links at 22.5px tall - which also duplicate the card link's href, adding 12 redundant tab stops. Filter chips and save buttons are correctly 44px+. The failing-contrast text is precisely the filter questions this person needs. Nothing in the thumb zone; no bottom bar.

**The cafe user with patchy signal.** No service worker, no `navigator.onLine` handling, no offline state, no install affordance anywhere in `src/` or `index.html`. Second visit on a dead connection is a blank page. `site.webmanifest` exists but nothing registers a worker; `start_url: "/"` plus `HashRouter` means the installed entry depends on a client-side redirect. Explicitly on DESIGN.md's backlog and outside the prototype phase - but the interface makes no admission of it.

**The painter matching paint to screen.** No violations - fidelity is genuinely protected. One thing to fix before it becomes one: `Exercises.tsx:78-81` applies `art-mat` then overrides its background with a pigment tint. Demos are not references so PRODUCT.md is not breached, but reusing the one class whose job is "never tinted" invites misuse on a reference later.

## Questions to Consider

1. What if Today had exactly one piece and no list at all? "More to try - 11 more" reintroduces the deliberation the product exists to remove.
2. If the constraint is time and energy, why is the first question "what do you want to paint"? What if the first screen were two taps - "How long?" and "How much energy?" - with the piece arriving after?
3. The screen is a reference surface, not a canvas. So why does it look like a page? What if the enlarge view were the default detail state - artwork edge to edge, metadata collapsed to a peelable strip?
4. What does the app do at minute three of a five-minute wash? Right now, nothing. The phone dims and the user has wet hands.
5. What would "no pressure" look like as a positive design move rather than the absence of streaks?
